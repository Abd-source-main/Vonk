# Two locale routes: `/` (Dutch) and `/en/`

The site is split into two complete, independently editable copies. There is no runtime
translation, no language library, no shared string table. **Editing Dutch copy never touches
English, and vice versa** — which is the whole point of the split, and the reason it is worth
the duplication.

Dutch is the *default* locale, so it is served straight from the site root. There is no
language chooser and no redirect in front of the homepage: `https://vonkelektra.nl/` is the
Dutch homepage itself. English is one directory down.

```
/     index.html              ← Dutch, primary market, x-default, THE homepage
      diensten.html
      over-ons.html
      contact.html
      privacybeleid.html
      algemene-voorwaarden.html
/en/  index.html              ← English
      services.html
      about.html
      contact.html
      privacy.html
      terms.html
/uploads/                     shared images
/support.js                   shared Claude Design runtime
```

Dutch is primary: the demand is local and Dutch-language (*elektricien Nijmegen*,
*zonnepanelen laten installeren*, *warmtepomp Nijmegen*). English exists for the
internationals and expats around Nijmegen — a real audience here, but the smaller one.

|-------|---------|-------------------------|
| `index.html` | `en/index.html` | `/` · `/en/` |
| `diensten.html` | `en/services.html` | `/diensten.html` · `/en/services.html` |
| `over-ons.html` | `en/about.html` | `/over-ons.html` · `/en/about.html` |
| `contact.html` | `en/contact.html` | `/contact.html` · `/en/contact.html` |
| `privacybeleid.html` | `en/privacy.html` | `/privacybeleid.html` · `/en/privacy.html` |
| `algemene-voorwaarden.html` | `en/terms.html` | `/algemene-voorwaarden.html` · `/en/terms.html` |

The two homepages canonicalise to their **directory** form — `/` and `/en/`, never
`/index.html` or `/en/index.html`. The in-page nav links still say `index.html`, because the
prototypes have to open over `file://`; a server resolves both to the same document and the
canonical settles which one is indexed. `verify-i18n.py` asserts the directory form.

Service anchors are localised too, so the Dutch URLs read as Dutch:

| English | Dutch |
|---------|-------|
| `#solar-panels` | `#zonnepanelen` |
| `#home-batteries` | `#thuisbatterij` |
| `#ev-charging` | `#laadpaal` |

| `#fuse-box` | `#meterkast` |
| `#fault-repair` | `#storingsdienst` |
| `#maintenance` | `#onderhoud` |

The image filenames under `uploads/` keep their English names on both routes. They are
assets, not URLs; renaming them would buy nothing and break both locales at once.

## Editing rules

**1. Edit one locale at a time, on purpose.**
The routes are allowed to drift where drift is correct. The Dutch storingsdienst page should
lean harder on urgency than the English one; the English pages can say more about working with
an installer in a country you are new to. Do not treat the English route as a mirror that must
match word for word.

**2. If you add a page, add it to both routes — or to neither.**
A page that exists in only one language is not a bug in itself, but it must then be left out of
the hreflang set entirely rather than pointed at a near-match. Pointing `hreflang="en"` at a
page that is not the English equivalent is worse than having no annotation.

**3. Whenever you touch a filename, canonical, or hreflang, run the checker.**

```
python seo/verify-i18n.py
```

It fails loudly on the five things that silently destroy hreflang:

- a missing self-referencing entry (Google discards the *entire* cluster for that page)
- a one-directional pair (dropped — if A names B, B must name A)
- a canonical that is not in its own hreflang set (all hreflang on the page ignored)
- HTML annotations disagreeing with `sitemap.xml` (conflicting pair dropped)
- broken local links

All of these fail silently in production. None of them show up by looking at the page.

**4. Never cross-canonical.**
`contact.html` canonicals to itself. It must never canonical to the English page, and the
English page must never canonical to Dutch. A cross-locale canonical suppresses the
non-canonical locale from the index completely, and it overrides hreflang when the two disagree.

**5. Keep the language switch pointing at the equivalent page, not the homepage.**
Someone reading the Dutch contact page who clicks the globe wants the English contact page.
Dumping them on the English homepage is the single most common i18n annoyance, and it is one
line of `href` to get right. All twelve switch links are currently correct and reciprocal, and
`verify-i18n.py` checks every one of them resolves.

The switch is the `.r-nav-lang` pill in the nav: a globe icon plus the code of the language it
takes you *to* (`EN` on Dutch pages, `NL` on English ones), with `title` and `aria-label`
written in that target language. Below 430px the code is hidden and the globe stands alone.
The two legal pages carry a plain worded link (`English` / `Nederlands`) in the footer instead,
because they have no nav.

## Decisions worth knowing

**The root is the Dutch homepage, not a chooser and not a redirect.** Visiting
`https://vonkelektra.nl/` serves `index.html` directly — one document, no hop. A chooser page
would put a thin, contentless interstitial in front of the primary market for every brand
search; a `301 / → /nl/` would put a redirect in front of every single visitor and every crawl
of the most important URL on the site. Serving the default locale from the root avoids both.

**The root does not sniff language.** No Accept-Language redirect, no IP redirect, on the root
or anywhere else. Googlebot crawls from US addresses and sends no Accept-Language header, so a
negotiated redirect would hide one locale from it entirely — and a visitor who wants the other
language should not have to fight the site for it. The globe in the nav is the way across, on
every page, in one click.

**`/nl/` is gone but still redirects.** The old two-folder layout was live long enough to be
crawled, so `seo/redirects/` keeps a `301 /nl/* → /*` rule alongside the `.dc.html` ones. Those
rules are the only ones in that directory that matter now; there is deliberately no rule for
`/`.

**x-default points at the Dutch page**, since the Netherlands is the primary market.

**One business entity, marked up once.** The `Electrician` JSON-LD graph is emitted only on
the Dutch `index.html`, under a single `@id`. Repeating it on the English homepage would assert two
businesses at two `@id`s, or the same `@id` twice with different `url` values. One entity, one
declaration.

**The `.dc.html` extension has been dropped.** It was a Claude Design artefact, never a URL
design; the files are plain `.html` now and the design canvas can still open them. The
remaining step toward the clean paths in `SITE-ARCHITECTURE.md` is directory URLs
(`/diensten/` rather than `/diensten.html`), and when that happens the canonicals, the hreflang
tags, `sitemap.xml` and `redirects/` all change together, in one pass — never one without the
others. That is the moment to also split the eight services into their own pages, which is the largest
single SEO win still on the table (see `SEO-AUDIT.md`, finding O1 and the plan in
`SITE-ARCHITECTURE.md`).

## Still to do on the Dutch route

The translation is complete and the structure is correct, but two things from the audit are
unchanged by the split and still block launch:

- **The reviews are placeholders**, and say so in the source. They are now placeholders in
  Dutch as well. Replace them with real, attributable reviews or remove the section — see
  `SEO-AUDIT.md`, finding C1.
- **The service imagery is `[ foto: ... ]` stand-ins.** Real photographs of Vonk's own jobs are
  also an E-E-A-T signal that stock photography cannot buy.

And one thing the split makes newly relevant: the Dutch H1s now carry the local keyword
(`Elektricien in Nijmegen voor wonen & werken in comfort.`), while the English ones keep the
original brand line. That is deliberate — the English route is not competing for
*elektricien Nijmegen* and should not try to.
